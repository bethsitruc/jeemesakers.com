Last Wednesday (September 10), my good friend Terry W. gave me a call.

"Charlie Kirk has been shot," he said.

"What?" I replied incredulously.

I turned the TV to news reports and have been watching since, trying to make sense of our country's latest act of political violence.

Let me say, from the onset, that I am a huge fan of Charlie Kirk. He spoke at our church in northern Virginia, and I have admired his outspoken defense of Christian values, his efforts to advance conservative viewpoints among young people, his bold defense of traditional marital lifestyles, and his willingness to engage in bold and open dialogue on college and university campuses. It was this latter effort, inviting students to express their opinions on a college campus in Utah that cost him his life; deprived a wife and two daughters of his future companionship; and robbed the conservative political movement among young people of its most articulate and effective spokesman.

I know some of you don't share this view.

Although, quite honestly, I struggle to figure out why.

Even in a world where we are bombarded from every corner by mainstream media outlets and social media platforms which screams hate screeds at each and every vestige of conservative thought, the post-shooting comments (including those directed against Kirk's widow and his two young children), have set new lows in sleeziness, if not displays of demonic conscience-searing. I'm beginning to think it is a much deeper problem, more than---as many of my friends claim---a personal antagonism for Donald Trump.

Those trying to explain the Charlie Kirk phenomenon seem to be floundering. Ian Ward---in trying to analyze why the political left has no equivalent to Charlie Kirk---described the recently assassinated political figure this way: "he served a dizzying array of functions within the broader Trumpian universe: a firebrand activist and debater; a media mega-personality overseeing a vast digital empire; the head of a multi-million-dollar political machine at *Turning Point USA*; a popularizer of the ideological fashions of the young and online right; an occasional adviser to Trump and his inner circle; and a bosom buddy with many of the most powerful denizens of the White House and Mar-a-Lago."<Footnote number={3} />

In my view, many miss the main point. Kirk's popularity stemmed from his commitment to his faith---out of which flowed his political views and unflinching stands on today's social issues. You cannot separate the two. He never failed to give credit to the Lord Jesus Christ. That logic simply defies worldly thinking and conventional human-based explanations for political occurrences. In a world where everyone has been saturated with lack of Absolute Truth, moral equivalency, and political correctness, Kirk's consistency of standing for Christ resonated with younger minds seeking a sense of certainty and meaning. That made him both genuine and unique and differentiated him from the camera-first stands of politicians on both sides of the aisle.

Look at the reactions that followed the shooting. Many have pointed out that Kirk's followers did ***not*** protest in the streets, loot stores, or stop traffic. They have conducted peaceful prayer vigils across the country and inquiries to Kirk's organization, *Turning Point USA,* have exploded exponentially. His widow, in a tearful address, pledged to carry on the organization's schedule of campus visits and online activities.

How have Kirk's opponents responded? Democrats made noisy interruptions in Congress when asked to recognize the tragedy with a moment of silence. Are you kidding me? Indeed, I have been amazed at the political vitriol and almost-demonic response of many on the far-left as they openly celebrate the death of Charlie Kirk and insult his faith and even his family.

Is this what we've come to?

Why are we here?

In my lifetime I have witnessed five of these mega-political-assassinations and close calls: John F. Kennedy on November 22, 1963 (which generated decades-long conspiracy theories); Martin Luther King's assassination in Memphis on April 4, 1968; Robert Kennedy's assassination on June 6, 1968; the attempt on Ronald Reagan's life on March 30, 1981; and the attempt on Donald Trump's life at an open-air campaign rally near Butler, Pennsylvania on July 13, 2024 (only a head twist away from becoming a martyr himself). I can remember where I was when I first heard the news of each of these. Do you?

What then, makes this one feel different?

I would respectfully suggest two things: technology and the uniqueness of Gen Z as a demographic cohort.

As to technology, news of Charlie Kirk's murder was disseminated within microseconds to every corner of the world. This is the first generation where that has been possible. In the world of 24-7 news coverage online and within the mainstream media, his gruesome murder was instantly available. The horror of the death-shot to the neck was replayed immediately and constantly online. Some have argued that the human psyche is ill-equipped to cope with this kind of instant, bloody, and violent tragedy.

Today, we carry in our pockets a communications tool that instantly connects us to the most inhuman crimes, in war and otherwise, complete with blood-saturated video images.

I write repeatedly about the social, political and spiritual challenges of exponentially changing technology in my missives.

The second factor, Gen Z, I have not addressed as often. Here I am referring to people born between 1996 and 2010, whose identity has been shaped by the digital age, media-induced climate anxiety, shifting financial landscapes, and the COVID-19 pandemic. Many journalists have tried to explain Charlie Kirk's extraordinary appeal to this college-aged cohort. Very few of them, of course, give any credit to the Lord Jesus Christ for this attraction. Two commentators, for example, tried to explain the phenomenon using a purely human-based rationale: they began with an 18-year-old Charlie Kirk bursting upon the scene out of nowhere. During those days, they explained further, his early idol was radio host Rush Limbaugh and borrowed the latter's conservative political philosophy of lower taxes and smaller government. By 2024, Charlie Kirk, using his skills as communicator and social networker, somehow moved from being an outsider to rubbing elbows with the likes of Tucker Carlson and Donald Trump Jr. As a result, a myth of sorts grew around him; although lacking a college education, he built an organization with 800 college chapters and millions of young followers. The organization raised over \$100 million for the last presidential election and spearheaded a "Get Out The Vote" effort that resulted in record numbers of young people voting for the Republican candidate. During these years, Charlie Kirk accomplished the unimaginable---he made the uncool cool on campus, and transformed college life by not just building a political movement, but becoming a lifestyle and a social and cultural identity.<Footnote number={4} />

Clearly, Charlie Kirk's primary target in these efforts was Gen Z, a generation which has proved to be more idiosyncratic and open to new perspectives than the media-meisters had predicted. He sensed that the timing was right for a countercultural response and worked to feed and cultivate that longing. To facilitate this desire, he offered open debating fora on campuses where campus classrooms had become closed to anything but politically correct thinking. Charlie Kirk had an intuitive sense of the Gen Z's worldview of social nativism and parasocial relationships. There was, in his view, a whole generation being "informed" by podcasters and influencers, a willingness to interpret life based on shows or specific online personalities rather than a sense of monoculture (the way it had always been done in the past).<Footnote number={5} />

So, on that fateful day on the Utah Valley campus as a crowd of approximately 3,000 young people gathered, two Gen Z motives collided: 31-year-old Charlie Kirk, sitting under a tent, inviting young people to a microphone to talk about the issues of the day and offer spiritual encouragement; and the other, 22-year-old Tyler Robinson, a Utah native, who sipped a much different digital Kool-Aid. Robinson, confused, a college drop-out, from a family of MAGA and pro-gun supporters, and constantly immersed in social media sites and computer games, had developed a hateful "obsession" about Charlie Kirk. Or at least this is the picture emerging from the ongoing investigation of Robinson's digital footprint, according to FBI sources.

Here is what is different. Robinson---as far as we can tell---was not moved by any coherent ideology nor, as far as that goes, inclined to make the event a mass murder of Charlie Kirk's followers. One shot, one target. He intended to get away with the crime. But why did he so carefully plan the event: why did he pull the trigger? We may never know for sure (especially as recent plea-bargaining arrangements in such high-profile murder cases have not required the killer to divulge their motives). More puzzling still is the trail of deliberately planted messages which Robinson left behind, such as carefully inscribed bullet casings with references to obscure online subcultures. These obscure messages contain references to memes, (that tend to be posted on more politically incorrect anonymous message boards or gamer chat apps), or incorrect gaming symbols and in-jokes only understandable to a niche community of online gamers.<Footnote number={6} />

Robinson apparently did it to earn their admiration and leave everyone else guessing.

This resulting veil of ambiguity further clouds Robinson's motive for the murder. Some claim this ambiguity may have been a deliberate strategy on his part. The result will almost certainly be a post-murder environment where conspiracy theories are certain to abound. A sampling of those that have already emerged: Robinson's links (if any) to a far-right Groyper movement which was apparently at odds with Charlie Kirk's brand of conservatism; links online to trans and homophobic subcultures; links to extreme LDS groups; leftist ideological online groups; and even pro-Zionist groups. As one commentator noted, now there is a jockeying of sorts with interested parties trying to label or disavow Robinson: in other words, the algorithmic internet is at work---"a justification machine where facts and news aren't so much presented and reported as they are catalogued and then rearranged to fit preset narratives."<Footnote number={7} /> Moreover, we should learn more about Robinson's conversations with his "transitioning" roommate, and perhaps as many as twenty others, on *Discord* (a messaging platform popular with the gaming community)<Footnote number={8} /> or connections with a growing constellation of fandoms, *i.e.,* a hybrid threat network of disaffected people who defy "normal" political ideologies and include, in part: Columbine obsessives, neo-Nazis, child groomers and trolls.<Footnote number={9} />

The boundaries between these fringe online denizens and real life are becoming increasingly blurry.

My friends it is a far different world out there (at least for me).

So, maybe we'd better get back to what we know for sure. Last week, Robinson was arrested---after a 33-hour manhunt---at his house and charged with aggravated murder, felony discharge of a firearm and obstruction of justice. Robinson had arrived on the Utah Valley College campus approximately one-half hour, prior to shooting a single bullet at Charlie Kirk at 12:20 p.m. The high velocity bullet tore into Charlie Kirk's neck, where the "conservative influencer" was pronounced dead at the hospital.<Footnote number={10} />

Photos of an incarcerated Robinson are expressionless with empty eyes.

Robinson was apprehended after his father recognized him from photographs and video of the crime scene released by authorities seeking public help in identifying the shooter. Robinson's father urged him to turn himself in, Robinson refused at first, but eventually relented after the intervention of a youth pastor (also a U.S. Marshal's task force officer).

So, what will happen in the post-Charlie Kirk world, especially among the younger generation? Good question. Of course there will be the media circus of a trial, probably taking place several months after a massive tribute and memorial planned for Charlie Kirk at the 64,300-seat State Farm Stadium on the morning of September 21. The size of the venue is a testimony to the influence of Charlie Kirk as a man, Christian influencer, and political hero to a young generation of followers.<Footnote number={11} />

Here is what I hope does not happen. I hope the present administration does not use the murder and ambiguity of motive as a pretext to attack its opponents on the left. I think that would be a big mistake. In my view, early statements by President Trump and other officials in this regard have not been helpful.

We need to start talking *to*---rather than shouting *at*---each other. If we don't take that away from this tragic episode, then Charlie Kirk's death will be in vain.

For me, at least, this investigation still has a long, long way to go.

Much more interesting, in my view, is how the movement goes forward. A promising start in this direction was the emotional tribute to Charlie Kirk by his widow (Erika Kirk) two days after her husband's death. She wanted to assure all those involved in her husband's organization that his work will go on: "no one will ever forget my husband's name," she said as she wiped tears from her eyes, "you have no idea the fire that you have ignited within this wife, the cries of this widow will echo around the world like a battle cry."

I pray God equips her for the battle that lies ahead.

In my view, the future of a generation---and a country---is at stake.
