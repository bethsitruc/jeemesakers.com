Several years ago, I attended a special meeting at the Ronald Reagan Building (the largest federal building within Washington D.C. proper). Before we could enter the building, we were all forced to make way for a visiting entourage. The principal person in the entourage was the then Queen of Jordan. There were no signs to designate her as a person of importance, no trumpeters to announce her arrival, but I could tell by her bearing---just the way she carried herself---that she belonged to royalty.

I think of that event often these days. In 2026, a year that promises to be a time of tumult and chaos, the desire of my heart is to carry and conduct myself like a King's Kid, an adopted member of God's royal family. This "title" is *not* by virtue of any special skill I bring to the table but solely because of what Jesus did for me at the cross. In this yearlong journey, I want to be "comfortable in my own spiritual skin," possess a special kind of inner peace, and continually practice the presence of God. Moreover, I want to conduct myself so that it is obvious to each member of my family, my friends, my business associates and my former students. I won't have to strut, to make noisy proclamations, or make others feel inferior in any way: I just want to act, think, and relate as a joint heir of the throne---a child adopted into the family of God---by virtue of what Jesus Christ has done for me.

I want to be a King's Kid!

Having said that, for the last several days I have been praying and thinking about this thought: what exactly is a King's Kid?

Here are a few of my ideas.

First and foremost, my *status* as a King's Kid is neither determined by my *circumstances* nor the circumstances of others around me. The controlling factor is my initial decision to follow the Lord Jesus Christ and my desire to dedicate my life---such as it is---to His Kingdom. On good days and bad days, in sickness or in health, in prosperity or counting pennies, in the mission field or here at home, I am a King's Kid.

That is *who* I am.

Second, walking as a King's Kid is a *spiritual attitude* rather than a self-improvement strategy. My manual for conduct and behavior is found in God's Word, not the reason or institutions of man. I live separate from the world yet a part of it. My sole desire is to please and serve the King and His Kingdom.

Third, living as a King's Kid is a lifelong process of maturing, growing and *becoming.* I'm accepted into the royal household by virtue of an individual decision; I learn to live as a King's Kid by studying to show myself approved (2 Timothy 2:15).

Fourth, the King Himself has graciously provided three signposts and help-aids to guide me in the way to walk as a King's Kid. The first is the roadmap provided by the Bible. The second is strategically planted mentors in my life; men and women of God who have the experience and spiritual maturity necessary to help guide my steps along the way. (One of my favorite passages in the Bible illustrates this point: young David was anointed to be the future king of Israel, but it took his relationship with Saul's son Jonathan to teach him the true ways of the royal court and how to be a king, 1 Sam. 18-20.) The third is the indwelling and guidance of the Holy Spirit (see John 14:15-18).

Fifth, as a King's Kid I have a special access to the throne as long as I approach with a spirit of thanksgiving (Phil. 4:6-7). I will have provisions for everything I need---*not everything I want*---to carry out my assignment as a King's Kid. I am neither a beggar nor an orphan.

Sixth, as a King's Kid I am obligated to take care of my body and treat it as the temple of the Holy Spirit (1 Cor. 6:19-20). I still have much to learn here.

Seventh, as a King's Kid I am not afraid to take on giants in my life. This can be a really tough area of spiritual growth. Each of us have our own giants to recognize, confront and overcome. Such experiences are necessary obstacles for every King's Kid; there are no exemptions or exceptions. (In this regard, last week, I went with Imogene and my sisters to see the animated, biblically themed musical *David*.<Footnote number={2} /> I was particularly interested in the way they portrayed the battle between David and Goliath, 1 Sam. 17-18.)

Finally, being a King's Kid does not entitle me to act like a spoiled brat. I am not to act in a way that brings undue attention to myself at the expense of my servanthood to the King. Being a King's Kid is a *privilege* not an *entitlement.*

I realize this missive is different in tone and style than most of my missives. But it is the most heartfelt. It is my experience that most of the individuals in my life who call themselves Christians are not living as true King's Kids. We are called to be walking spiritual atomic bombs, not whimpering, groveling weaklings. It is my hope that as this new year unfolds, each of you take a long look in the mirror and see yourself as the Lord sees you: a potential child of the King.

A King's Kid!

Blessings,

Jeemes
