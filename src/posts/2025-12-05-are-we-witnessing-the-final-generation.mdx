The origin of this missive goes back almost a decade, much of it lifted from the introduction of a book I intended to write called *A Simple Question.* That the logic and spiritual truths still hold up as we approach the end of 2025, is a testimony---at least to me---of the value of my original assertions.

But I'll let you decide.

Back then, in 2016 or so, during one of our many discussions in our government class at the College of the Ozarks (CofO),<Footnote number={3} /> I shared my conviction with the students that they were likely a part of the final generation and that time, as we know it, was getting short. One of my students, Uliks Bokshi---a young man brought up in Kosovo---raised his hand. "Did you say the same things, about a final generation, to your college classes twenty years ago?"

It was a simple question.

Yet for some reason, that simple question penetrated to my inner core. As a result, I spent the entire following weekend in self-reflection, repeatedly asking myself, *how sure am I that that these kids, these millennials*<Footnote number={4} /> *in my college classroom, and my grandchildren by blood, mostly Gen-Zers, are really the final generation? Are these individuals actually living in the final days? If so, what will the next fifteen to twenty years (assuming things hold together that long) hold for them?*

After all, the argument goes, haven't believers been saying they were the final generation since the days of the early church?

In this vein, I ran across a fascinating article written by one of my favorite futurists---and a self-acknowledged Christian---Kevin Kelly,<Footnote number={5} /> suggesting that Christians may want to prepare themselves to be around for another 1,000 years (only 13 generations):

> "The problem with Christianity is that for the past 1,000 years it has permitted itself only one scenario of the future: the end of the world in our lifetime. In fact, make that 2,000 years. The first generations of Christians believed the future was short lived, and that it would end in their lifetimes. And the 25 lifetime-generations since then have also believed that the future was short, and the world would end in their lifetime. Every generation has legitimately seen signs and wonders indicating an immediate end of the world. The scenario was plausible in every century. It might still happen today, in our lifetime."

But then, in the very next paragraph, Kelly presents a thought that is far more controversial:

> "The end of the world in our lifetime is a scenario that cannot be rejected. It is as plausible as it has ever been. But in order to fashion a Christianity ready for the next 1,000 years, this generation of Christians must begin to create alternative future scenarios in order to fill out the space of possibilities. By relying on a single scenario of the future for the last 2,000 years, in particular a single scenario that was constantly and decidedly wrong, Christianity left the invention and control of the actual future to those outside the church. Relatively few scientists are Christians, and almost no futurists are. By retreating to this unwavering single wrong prediction Christianity has surrendered the future to non-believers."<Footnote number={6} />

Kelly, speaking on another occasion, noted that using pure mathematical probabilities it is far more likely that Jesus Christ will return in 1,000 years than tomorrow.

That thought gives me pause for reflection.

Is it possible that we are not living in the times of the final generation? Absolutely. Is it possible that key biblical passages pointing to a prophetic future will gain an entirely new meaning if time lingers on? Yes, absolutely.<Footnote number={7} />

Why am I so sure then, that today's generation of young people will be part of the final generation?

Almost forty years ago (can it possibly be that long?), I taught similar college history and philosophy classes to a different generation of students on a different campus. Both groups of students were in similar age groups but over three decades apart in time-space. Were my warnings of an impending end-of-time the same to those former students? If so, I missed the mark with my warnings---no matter how sincere---four decades ago. Knowing that, how can I be certain that today's warnings to my readers are any more valid?

Moreover, how would I have responded to such a simple question posed by a student in my classes in those days before the turn of the millennium?

So, here it goes. There are four basic reasons I am more convinced than ever that today's generation of young people are living in the final days. The first is deeply personal; a nagging, inner spiritual sensation that is giving rise these days to an increasing sense of *urgency*. In many respects, that is the real crux of the matter. I definitely lacked the same sense of *urgency* with my college students a decade ago and certainly almost four decades ago.

Why?

Perhaps that is what bothers me most. If indeed we are edging perilously close to the end of time, why is there such a complete lack of urgency in our churches? Why is there such blindness in our culture and society to the consequences of the spiritual deterioration everywhere around us?

Why aren't the students and others around me feeling it?

Are my grandchildren being equipped to sense it?

What is blinding them to this spiritual reality?

At any rate, it was this personal sense of urgency that motivated my classroom activities at CofO almost a decade ago now; I simply *had* to warn a new crop of college students of the shortness of time.

As I think about it now---sitting here in December 2025---my sense of urgency during those days was driven, in large part, by a personal vision. The vision is the second reason I am convinced today's generation of young people are approaching the final threshold of time. As I have explained to others, I don't have many visions, so this one I had in 2015 or so has stayed fresh in my mind. For the first time, I shared the details of this vision with my first crop of CofO college students. I don't know how many of you readers have had a life-changing vision. It is certainly not a sign that I'm anyone special. The vision was to equip me for an assignment. For that reason, the power and freshness of the vision still motivates me.

The vision? There I was, somehow, walking in the halls of the heavenly realm watching the great assembly from afar when I noticed *them,* the final generation---the *remnant---*I write so much about, prominently seated in the throng of believers gathered below.

They were adorned in blazing white robes.

They were the ultimate overcomers of end-time challenges.

Sprinkled among them were the faces of my students, past, present and future. But only a small number of them.

I sensed that each of these final remnant individuals I envisioned ***had*** to seek God's wisdom to survive the technological tsunami and counterfeit truth that is coming. And it *is* coming. (Perhaps it is worth noting that the vision preceded Covid-19, the pervasive growth of artificial intelligence (AI), and biogenetic developments.) At any rate, the spiritual reality that some of my students---and hopefully, grandchildren---were present among this special gathering was a confirming testimony to me that some of them would overcome the perils that would lie ahead.

That vision still gives me some measure of comfort in today's world which has, for all intents and purposes, gone crazy and all indicators point to it getting worse over the days ahead.

The third reason underscoring my conviction that today's young generation is living in the end-times is that today's unique technological developments are coming ever closer to fulfilling the last-days' biblical prophecies by Jesus Christ, the revelatory vision of Saint John and the Old Testament prophets. I would respectfully suggest that no previous generation can make the same claim in the same way. I have written about this extensively in my previous missives and suspect this trendline will continue in future missives.

The fourth reason is a bit more complex to explain. In today's world, our young people will need the reality of a close relationship with Jesus Christ to survive and become overcomers in the days ahead. The key is entering into a personal relationship and not just participating in a "religion" with its formalized, manmade rules. This spiritual reality is true regardless of whether Jesus returns tomorrow, ten years from now, twenty years from now, or 1,000 years from now. Today, the innocent, and in most cases, unwitting minds of the young people are clouded increasingly by constant social media use, worldly agendas and elite-driven narratives. If our young peoples' ears are beginning to resonate to the *noise* and strategies of deception today, imagine what it will be decades from now.

Based on what I see around me, young people by and large are totally *unprepared spiritually for what is coming*.

I see this as my assignment during this window, this *season,* of my life, to warn and equip those young people willing to listen and heed the warning.

Furthermore, I believe that today's deception and *noise*---now only in the beginning stages---is being orchestrated *deliberately* to confuse, annul and supplant three exclusive claims made by Jesus Christ about Himself:

> "*Jesus said to him, I am the **Way** and the **Truth** and the **Life;** no one comes to the Father except by (through) me."*<Footnote number={8} />

This exclusive, non-politically-correct claim to deity led those religious and political elites around Jesus to crucify Him. Everything we believe is based in His perfect sacrifice on the cross. Yet, in today's politically correct culture, one advocating the exclusivity of Jesus Christ likewise will be ostracized, ridiculed and cast out.

Try it out yourself.

Next time you find yourself in a worldly collection of family, friends, co-workers or religious colleagues, take a bold stand for Jesus Christ as the sole gateway of eternal life. Watch the reaction. If, on the other hand, you are among those in the group when someone else makes such a bold statement, ask yourself why such a claim triggers such an uneasy feeling inside you. If there is no validity to Jesus' claims, why this uncomfortable response?

Plainly and simply, I accept the messianic claim of Jesus Christ at face value.

Of course, this assertion flies in the face of a growing legion of futurists, technocrats, elites, hi-tech titans, media types, politicians, economists and college professors who seek to counterfeit and undermine Jesus' trinity of claims---that He is the *only* way, the *only* truth and the *only* life.

I want to make my agenda clear from the beginning.

Finally, in my mind, researching and putting together the ideas necessary to establish the truth that we are indeed living in the final days resembles, it seems to me, the process of working a giant jigsaw picture puzzle. Imagine a huge, larger-than-life puzzle that is laid out on a vast table before each of us with each of the pieces extremely difficult to fit in place. But it is much more than a game: I---we---*must* complete the puzzle so this generation can see the final picture.

It is my---our---legacy.

Yet, to this point, I have only finished the border and small portions of the rest of the puzzle. Indeed, I have put together just enough puzzle pieces to have a vague idea of what the final picture will look like. More than once, something or somebody has come along and bumped the table, either accidentally or intentionally, which impedes my progress. I've also had to constantly rework portions of the puzzle on which I've already spent considerable time and mental energy. New puzzle pieces are constantly being added to the table to be fit in, and several important pieces have fallen on the floor.

Moreover, the only puzzle-box picture to guide me---the Bible---all too often lies neglected on the couch or table.

The partially completed puzzle I am looking at now is much different than the puzzle I originally started working, although many of the pieces are the same.

To make matters worse, I am running out of time to complete the puzzle.

Previously, I shared with some of you earlier versions of the incomplete puzzle picture, in the form of my future "assumptions." These assumptions---the early puzzle pieces---were originally intended as a project to lay out background information for my three fiction novels.<Footnote number={9} />

Now I am in the process of reconfiguring the puzzle parts.

Certainly, the project has proven to be infinitely more difficult, and complex, than I originally thought.

My more recent thoughts and writings---composed in response to recently arrived puzzle pieces---have morphed into an entirely new picture. And even that puzzle picture is constantly changing.

Nevertheless, this corner of the puzzle that I have been recently preoccupied with, still remains, somehow, shaped by the urgency of a well-meaning simple question asked over a decade ago, a vision in the night, breathtaking modern technological changes, and the three extraordinary claims of Jesus Christ about Himself.

The bottom line: sitting here at the puzzle table, I remain convinced that *time,* as we know it, is running short.
